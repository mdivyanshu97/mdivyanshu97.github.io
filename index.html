Senior Research Scientist Amazon Science

I am a Senior Research Scientist at Amazon, where I work on post-training LLMs and agentic systems. My research focuses on reasoning, planning, and tool use in LLMs and agents, with an emphasis on improving accuracy, confidence calibration, and robustness. I aim to develop reliable, computationally efficient systems that can handle complex, multi-step tasks at Amazon’s scale.

I am also a Visiting Researcher at the University of Oxford, where I work on multimodal post-training, video representation learning, and world models. My research interests include developing efficient, temporally aware video representations that capture long-range temporal context, as well as learning and aligning representations across modalities with minimal reliance on paired data.

I completed my DPhil in Computer Vision and Machine Learning at the University of Oxford, supervised by Professor Alison Noble and supported by the Athena-Bronze Scholarship. My doctoral research focused on learning from long-form videos, particularly fetal ultrasound, to support the identification of congenital heart disease. This work spanned temporal localization, self-supervised learning, and multimodal representation learning.

Before joining Amazon full-time, I interned at Amazon Science as an Applied Scientist II, where I investigated whether Video-LLMs could learn temporal reasoning entirely from text and transfer these capabilities to video at inference time. This work explored the modality gap between text and visual representations, and how cross-modal alignment can enable transfer without relying on paired video-text data for training.

Prior to Oxford, I worked as a Data Scientist at the Translational Health Science and Technology Institute (THSTI), Government of India, with Professor Shinjini Bhatnagar. In collaboration with clinicians and public health researchers, I developed machine learning methods for preterm birth prediction, gestational-age estimation, and privacy-preserving ultrasound analysis, some of which are now undergoing clinical validation and translation.

I welcome discussions on research, new ideas, and potential collaborations.
